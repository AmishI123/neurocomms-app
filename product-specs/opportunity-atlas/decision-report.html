Product choice · engineering evidence · no participant claim

Build the communication-access card first

The project has three sensible first products. The card is the only one with a working implementation and a complete checked flow today.

Atlas presentation · Product comparison · End-to-end goal

Decision

Build and review the communication-access card before starting the reading desk or time-step helper. This is an engineering sequence decision, not a ranking of people’s needs or proof that people will use the card.

The first task is: make a short card for one real-life situation, edit the words, hide anything private, preview the exact shared content, save it on the device, and export only after review.

Why this product comes first

  • A working prototype and a clear product requirements document already exist.
  • The task is small enough to test from opening to export without building a whole communication system.
  • The person writes and approves the content. The app does not require a diagnosis or guess what the person means.
  • The same basic flow may be useful in several settings, while still allowing personal wording and different communication methods.
  • Its risks are visible and bounded: privacy of printed copies, wording fit, and the fact that another person may still refuse to listen.

Exact first task

Choose a situation

Use a healthcare visit, school meeting, work conversation, public service, or another situation chosen by the person. The card does not ask for a diagnosis.

Start blank or from an example

The example is only a starting point. Every sentence can be changed, hidden, reordered, or removed.

Write the person’s words

Include the communication method, time needed, turn-taking preference, and what to do when the message is unclear.

Check the public view

The preview shows only visible, non-empty content. Private editor notes do not appear automatically.

Save deliberately

Save only on the device when the person chooses. Reload and confirm that the card is still there. Delete it with a clear confirmation.

Review before sharing

Show the exact text that will leave the browser. Then print, export, or use the device share option. If sharing is unavailable, keep a local text download.

What was checked now

The current release audit passed 22 of 22 browser and decision-flow checks. This includes the feature, acceptance, accessibility, keyboard, accessibility-tree, host-boundary, selected-task, and review-protocol checks at the documented browser-library setting.

  • Accessible page structure and labelled controls.
  • Editable example content and live preview.
  • Hidden statements excluded from preview and export review.
  • Explicit review before text and HTML export.
  • Save, reload, and delete in local browser storage.
  • Keyboard focus and 320px layout without horizontal overflow.

What this result does not show

There were no participants in this slice. The result does not show that people prefer the card, that a listener will respect it, or that it improves healthcare, school, work, or relationships. It does not show clinical benefit, demand, or suitability for every communication method.

The next test should compare the card with the person’s current paper or notes method on the same fictional task first. The prepared review protocol defines that comparison. Any later review must be voluntary, accessible, and led by the person’s chosen way of communicating.

Change-direction rules

  • Continue if the first task works and people say the card gives useful control for a situation.
  • Revise if the flow works but wording, steps, sharing, or access methods add avoidable effort.
  • Change direction if the existing method is easier with the same result, or if people do not want to show a card in the situation.
