# Comparison scan: additional areas

This document records the initial scan that led to the five detailed comparison pages. It is not a full evidence review; the linked HTML pages are the current product-research synthesis and retain the same evidence limits.

## Developmental language disorder

Developmental language disorder (DLD) affects how a person learns and uses language. The difficulty can involve understanding, finding words, building sentences, telling stories, or following complex language. It may continue into adult life even when the person appears to speak fluently.

The strongest product signal is participation: understanding forms and instructions, explaining a need, making decisions, navigating services, and taking part in independent living. A 2025 qualitative adult study reported low public understanding of DLD and effects on daily activities. A 2024 participatory project with young adults focused on the move toward independent living and making their voices heard in everyday services.

**Recommendation:** keep DLD as a high-priority comparison area and consider a full page later. The clearest first product question is: how can information and service steps be made understandable while allowing the person to answer in their own words?

Sources: [adult lived-experience study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27725383251368772), [participatory independent-living study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10443894231185193).

## Dyscalculia

Dyscalculia is a learning difficulty involving number understanding and calculation. The adult evidence points beyond school mathematics: people may struggle with money, time, measurement, quantities, codes, and planning when information must be processed quickly.

The product opportunity is practical number access. It could help a person check a bill, understand time until an appointment, compare quantities, count money, or make a plan using visual and physical references. It must support the person’s decision rather than take control of money or hide the calculation.

**Recommendation:** keep dyscalculia as a strong separate research lane. It is distinct from dyslexia because the main access problem concerns quantity, number, time, and measurement rather than written language, although the two can occur together.

Sources: [adult participation study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27546330251406911), [early-adulthood daily numeracy study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8946289/), [public adult discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/dyscalculia/comments/1f2383n).

## Developmental coordination disorder / dyspraxia

Developmental coordination disorder (DCD), also called dyspraxia in some communities, affects movement and coordination. Recent adult research describes difficulty with cooking, driving, household tasks, health management, shopping, bureaucracy, and financial tasks. Some tasks take more effort even when the person understands exactly what they want to do.

The product opportunity is action access: help with physical sequencing, positioning, handwriting alternatives, navigation, preparation, and asking for practical support. A purely cognitive task list will not solve a motor or environment problem.

**Recommendation:** keep DCD as a separate research lane, but involve occupational-therapy expertise before building a physical or motor-support intervention. A low-risk first test could focus on preparing a task or documenting which physical conditions help.

Sources: [young-adult participation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976185/), [adult lived-experience study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13218497/), [higher-education study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11675472/).

## Intellectual disability

The strongest product signal is accessible information and supported decision-making. People may need information broken into smaller parts, clear words, more time, communication support, and a trusted person who helps without deciding for them.

The product opportunity is a decision-support workspace: show choices in an accessible form, record the person’s own questions and preferences, allow changes, and make it clear who helped and what the person decided.

**Recommendation:** keep intellectual disability as a high-priority communication and autonomy lane. It should be studied with self-advocates and people who use supported communication. Do not make a caregiver dashboard the centre of the product.

Sources: [decision-making review](https://revistes.udg.edu/pedagogia_i_treball_social/article/view/22999), [supported decision-making focus groups](https://openalex.org/W4402852996), [accessible health decision-making study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jir.70165), [accessible communication report](https://inclusioncanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Advancing-Accessible-Communication-for-People-with-Intellectual-Disabilities-Final-Research-ReportMarch-2024-2.pdf).

## Aphasia

Aphasia is acquired communication loss, often after stroke or another brain injury. It belongs in the comparison because communication access, word finding, reading, writing, privacy, and partner behaviour matter. It should remain separate from developmental conditions because onset, recovery, age, medical context, and service pathways differ.

The product opportunity is communication recovery and participation support: accessible health information, conversation support, personal word and message tools, and partner guidance. A product must not assume that a person’s current ability is fixed or that a caregiver can speak for them.

**Recommendation:** retain aphasia as a separate clinically distinct lane. It is relevant to AAC and communication access, and now has a detailed exploratory page, but it still needs more primary and lived-experience evidence before product requirements are treated as settled.

The initial scan found a 2025 conference programme focused on communication access and social participation for people with aphasia and other communication disabilities. This is a lead for the next source pass rather than enough evidence for requirements.

Source lead: [Nordic Aphasia Congress 2025 abstract book](https://www.nordicaphasia.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/58/2025/05/NAC-2025-Abstract-book-according-to-schedule.pdf).
