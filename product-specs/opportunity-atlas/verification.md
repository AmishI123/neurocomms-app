# Opportunity atlas verification

## Checked on 15 September 2026

### Deliverables present

- Seven reader-facing HTML pages: index, four main area pages, comparison, and methods/evidence.
- Shared stylesheet.
- 37 claim records in `evidence-ledger.json`.
- 27 main-area source records in `sources.json`.
- 10 comparison-area source records in `comparison-sources.json`.
- `research-summary.md`, `research-log.md`, `comparison-scan.md`, `theme-coding.md`, and `open-questions.md`.

### Page checks

- All seven pages have one `h1` title.
- All four main pages have a representation note, five or six pain points, one everyday example per pain point, one proposed solution per pain point, one acceptance check per pain point, evidence links, and a non-goals section.
- All four main pages have an explicit product shortlist with at least three possible products.
- Five comparison pages now have the same detailed structure: DLD, dyscalculia, DCD, intellectual disability, and aphasia.
- Every comparison page labels its evidence population, product hypothesis, first safe test, and evidence boundary.
- All local links return HTTP 200 through the local server.
- Internal anchors resolve.
- The pages have no horizontal overflow at 1280px, 390px, or 320px viewport widths.
- The page JSON files parse successfully.

### Evidence boundary

The atlas is an initial evidence-backed product opportunity set. The public-post and search-selected sources are signals, not prevalence estimates. The proposed products are hypotheses. No page claims diagnosis, treatment, clinical benefit, user demand, or market demand.

### Known limitations and follow-up work

- The source pass is not yet exhaustive.
- Down syndrome and AAC need more direct self-advocate and AAC-user evidence.
- Dyslexia needs more adult workplace and higher-education task evidence.
- Aphasia needs a dedicated primary-study review before a full page is created.
- The pages need a human review by people with relevant lived experience before a product direction is selected.
