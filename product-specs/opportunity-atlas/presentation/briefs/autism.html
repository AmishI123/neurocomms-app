My conversation card

Autism: a complete proposed product brief in plain English.

15 September 2026 · Prepared from the saved project evidence. Product plans are proposed requirements, not tested outcomes.

1. Who this is for

An adult who wants to tell another person what makes a conversation easier.

This brief focuses on autistic adults who want more control over the pace, setting, or form of a conversation.

2. The problem and its evidence

The setting can use up attention

Noise, light, crowds, and uncertainty may make a conversation harder before it starts.

Questions can arrive too quickly

People may need time to understand a question, prepare an answer, and return to something missed.

Service steps can be hard to follow

Phone calls, forms, referrals, and changing instructions can add work around the actual appointment.

People can misread each other

A short answer or a delayed reply can be misunderstood. Both people and the setting affect the exchange.

Fictional everyday example

An adult arrives at a clinic with three questions. The waiting room is loud. They want written questions and a quieter place, but explaining that is already difficult.

The saved evidence includes adult healthcare studies and public discussions. It gives less coverage to children, people who use communication aids, and people who cannot take part in text-based research.

3. Three products we could create

ConceptWhat it doesTradeoff
My conversation cardWrite and show a short request.Small enough to test with the existing app; another person still has to respond.
Appointment notesPrepare questions and review answers after a visit.Useful for longer conversations; more private content to manage.
Service stepsTrack one referral or application.Could reduce admin work; service details need a named person to keep them current.

Proposed first version: My conversation card. The project already has a working communication-access card. Reviewing its wording and full sharing flow is a practical next step.

4. What the first version includes

The first version supports one complete task: An adult who wants to tell another person what makes a conversation easier. The screens below define its scope.

Deferred or excluded: Emotion guessing, gaze scoring, automatic disclosure, recording the other person, and behaviour grades.

5. Screen-by-screen plan

1

Choose a situation

Start blank or choose work, a visit, or a public service. These choices only change editable example text.

2

Write the request

Offer “Please ask one question at a time,” “Written information helps,” and an empty line. Allow edit, hide, reorder, and delete.

3

Show the card

Display only selected lines in large text. Keep private notes out of this view. Provide a clear return button.

4

Keep or share

Offer save on this device, print, or download after an exact preview. Explain that a recipient can keep a copy.

6. Product features and checks

IDRequired behaviourHow to check it
A-01Every suggested sentence is editableReplace all examples with a custom request; no diagnosis is required.
A-02Hidden lines stay privatePut a private line in the editor. Confirm it is absent from display, print, copy, and downloads.
A-03Switch requests for the situationChange a noise request to a request for more time without changing saved private notes.
A-04A failed save is visibleSimulate blocked storage. Keep current text available and offer a download without saying it was saved.

These are requirements for a future implementation. A listed check has not passed merely because it appears here.

7. Rules every first version must meet

IDRequirementExpected behaviour
C-01Keep control of sharingShow exactly what will be printed, copied, or downloaded. Hidden notes never appear. Sending remains a separate user action.
C-02Explain savingShow “Saved on this device,” “Not saved,” or a specific save error. A browser on a shared device is not a private account.
C-03Handle lost or damaged saved dataKeep the app usable if stored data is malformed. Offer a fresh start and preserve any current unsaved text for download.
C-04Make deletion clearDistinguish removing an item from clearing the whole saved project. Offer undo for the current action; clear that undo copy when the session ends. Explain that exported copies remain outside the app.
C-05Make the flow usableCheck keyboard navigation, visible focus, labelled fields, 200% zoom, and a narrow phone screen. Use text as well as colour for status.
C-06Keep examples optionalStart blank or edit examples. No required name, diagnosis, employer, or explanation for stopping.
C-07Keep data on the deviceThe first version has no accounts, analytics, or remote model calls. Check any speech feature separately before making an offline or privacy promise.
C-08Handle repeat actionsRepeated Save or Download clicks must not change content or create contradictory status. Returning from preview preserves the draft.

8. Information, saving, and privacy

Optional title, chosen sentences, display order, and hidden/shown status. Store a card only when the user chooses Save.

Keep temporary work in memory until the user chooses Save. In version one, saved work remains until the person deletes it or the browser clears it; say this clearly. Closing the page must release temporary drafts and undo copies. Do not promise secure erasure from the device or recall of printed and shared files.

Use a versioned file format for downloads intended for reopening. If reopening files is included, validate the format and ask before replacing an open draft. Treat imported text as text so it cannot run as page code.

9. When something goes wrong

If the device cannot speak or share, showing the text and downloading a file must still work. If the recipient ignores the card, the app can offer a chosen stop message; it cannot enforce an accommodation.

Cancellation returns to the same draft. An empty field gets a specific explanation where it is required; optional fields remain optional. Losing connection must not discard text already entered. Test the exact offline behaviour promised by the selected build.

10. How we would test usefulness

Can the person show the intended request and keep a private line hidden? Does making the card take less effort than writing the same request in ordinary notes?

  • Make a card for an unfamiliar visit.
  • Change one request while keeping a private note hidden.
  • Print and reopen the card; check that only intended lines appear.

First run the fictional tasks as engineering checks. Then prepare an accessible review with intended users and any support they choose. Compare with their existing method. Record task result, errors, help required, and effort in their own words. Choose the first review group for varied relevant needs and report its actual size; no participant results exist for these briefs.

Continue only if the intended task works and the person finds a benefit worth the added effort. Revise confusing wording or steps. Change direction if an existing tool works as well with less effort.

Planning estimate: Small extension of existing work. This is a relative scope judgment, not a time or budget quote. Review the existing app against this brief before adding a second implementation.

12. Who owns it and how it might reach people

The person controls the card. A supporter can help write it with their permission. Clinics or employers are possible distribution partners; demand and payment are untested.

Before estimating a business case, ask who currently handles this task, what they use, how often they need help, who would maintain the tool, and whether anyone would pay. The saved public posts do not answer those questions.

13. What could change the plan

Will people choose to show a card, and will the recipient make the requested change?

Also test whether preparation is too much work, the chosen access method fits, and another person or service must change for the product to help. A software fix may cover only part of the problem.
