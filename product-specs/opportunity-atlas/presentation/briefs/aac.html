My communication backup

Communication aids (AAC): a complete proposed product brief in plain English.

15 September 2026 · Prepared from the saved project evidence. Product plans are proposed requirements, not tested outcomes.

1. Who this is for

A person who uses communication aids and wants a chosen backup for a specific setting.

AAC means augmentative and alternative communication: ways to add to or replace speech, such as writing, signs, pictures, boards, or a speaking device. It is not a diagnosis.

2. The problem and its evidence

The device may be out of reach

Position, weather, battery, or a breakdown can interrupt access.

A fixed list can limit the message

People need ways to say something new, correct a listener, and refuse.

Conversation partners matter

A tool helps less when the other person will not wait or accept the communication method.

Private messages need protection

A shared screen or saved vocabulary can reveal personal information.

Fictional everyday example

A person’s usual device is charging during an outing. They need a few chosen messages and a way to tell a listener that none of those messages is what they mean.

The saved evidence mixes user accounts, research, and views from supporters and professionals. Device access differs widely. A generic backup cannot promise the same communication as a person’s usual system.

3. Three products we could create

ConceptWhat it doesTradeoff
My communication backupPrint or save selected messages for a device problem.Useful small scope if it works with the person’s access method.
New-message helperCompose messages beyond prepared phrases.Potentially valuable but risks duplicating or disrupting established systems.
Repair and support recordKeep model details, support contacts, and repair steps.Useful service task; information needs updating and does not restore access by itself.

Proposed first version: My communication backup. A small selected backup avoids importing an entire private vocabulary and can be compared with existing paper backups.

4. What the first version includes

The first version supports one complete task: A person who uses communication aids and wants a chosen backup for a specific setting. The screens below define its scope.

Deferred or excluded: Replacing a prescribed or chosen AAC system, guessing intended speech, universal vocabulary import, and emergency reliability claims.

5. Screen-by-screen plan

1

Choose backup content

Start empty and add selected messages, letters, or images. Explain that a backup has limits.

2

Choose the layout

Set text size, spacing, page size, and ordering. Keep a stable layout once used.

3

Practice using it

Show a listener guide chosen by the user and a way to say “None of these” or correct a selection.

4

Print or keep a copy

Preview exactly what leaves the device. Keep personal notes out of the copy.

6. Product features and checks

IDRequired behaviourHow to check it
AAC-01Select content explicitlyAdd five messages without importing unrelated vocabulary or conversation history.
AAC-02Provide a way to reject choicesReach “None of these” from the main board without navigating a hidden menu.
AAC-03Preserve agreed positionsReopen the board and confirm no message has moved automatically.
AAC-04Keep output faithfulPrinted and downloaded copies contain exactly the chosen content and ordering.

These are requirements for a future implementation. A listed check has not passed merely because it appears here.

7. Rules every first version must meet

IDRequirementExpected behaviour
C-01Keep control of sharingShow exactly what will be printed, copied, or downloaded. Hidden notes never appear. Sending remains a separate user action.
C-02Explain savingShow “Saved on this device,” “Not saved,” or a specific save error. A browser on a shared device is not a private account.
C-03Handle lost or damaged saved dataKeep the app usable if stored data is malformed. Offer a fresh start and preserve any current unsaved text for download.
C-04Make deletion clearDistinguish removing an item from clearing the whole saved project. Offer undo for the current action; clear that undo copy when the session ends. Explain that exported copies remain outside the app.
C-05Make the flow usableCheck keyboard navigation, visible focus, labelled fields, 200% zoom, and a narrow phone screen. Use text as well as colour for status.
C-06Keep examples optionalStart blank or edit examples. No required name, diagnosis, employer, or explanation for stopping.
C-07Keep data on the deviceThe first version has no accounts, analytics, or remote model calls. Check any speech feature separately before making an offline or privacy promise.
C-08Handle repeat actionsRepeated Save or Download clicks must not change content or create contradictory status. Returning from preview preserves the draft.

8. Information, saving, and privacy

Selected backup messages, optional images or letter board, layout settings, and user-approved listener instructions. No conversation log.

Keep temporary work in memory until the user chooses Save. In version one, saved work remains until the person deletes it or the browser clears it; say this clearly. Closing the page must release temporary drafts and undo copies. Do not promise secure erasure from the device or recall of printed and shared files.

Use a versioned file format for downloads intended for reopening. If reopening files is included, validate the format and ask before replacing an open draft. Treat imported text as text so it cannot run as page code.

9. When something goes wrong

If a device, printer, or access method cannot use the layout, the review fails. Do not describe unsupported switch or eye-gaze use as working. Local deletion cannot recall copies already shared.

Cancellation returns to the same draft. An empty field gets a specific explanation where it is required; optional fields remain optional. Losing connection must not discard text already entered. Test the exact offline behaviour promised by the selected build.

10. How we would test usefulness

Can the user convey a chosen message, reject an incorrect interpretation, and use the agreed backup when the main device is unavailable?

  • Prepare a backup containing a refusal and a custom message.
  • Correct a listener who picked the wrong item.
  • Use the printed copy with the usual device set aside.

First run the fictional tasks as engineering checks. Then prepare an accessible review with intended users and any support they choose. Compare with their existing method. Record task result, errors, help required, and effort in their own words. Choose the first review group for varied relevant needs and report its actual size; no participant results exist for these briefs.

Continue only if the intended task works and the person finds a benefit worth the added effort. Revise confusing wording or steps. Change direction if an existing tool works as well with less effort.

11. Build order and responsibilities

StepWorkDone when
1. Check fitResearch lead checks the sources and reviews the proposed task with intended users.The real task, missing evidence, and comparison method are recorded.
2. Draw the four screensDesigner writes all labels and prepares paper or clickable examples.A reader can describe what each action does and how to stop.
3. Build the core flowDeveloper implements the four screens and product requirements.Every product feature check above has a recorded result.
4. Finish storage and accessDeveloper and reviewer check saving, errors, keyboard use, print, and sharing.Common checks pass and known limitations are stated.
5. Review and decideResearch lead reports user review separately from engineering tests.The decision says continue, revise, or stop, with reasons.

Planning estimate: Medium; access-method support can increase effort. This is a relative scope judgment, not a time or budget quote. Review with AAC users and specialists, including people who do not use touch input.

12. Who owns it and how it might reach people

The communication user owns the messages and layout. A specialist may help match access methods. Device services could distribute it; export compatibility needs checking.

Before estimating a business case, ask who currently handles this task, what they use, how often they need help, who would maintain the tool, and whether anyone would pay. The saved public posts do not answer those questions.

13. What could change the plan

What important messages or access methods are lost when moving to the backup?

Also test whether preparation is too much work, the chosen access method fits, and another person or service must change for the product to help. A software fix may cover only part of the problem.
