My words for today

Down syndrome: a complete proposed product brief in plain English.

15 September 2026 · Prepared from the saved project evidence. Product plans are proposed requirements, not tested outcomes.

1. Who this is for

An adult who wants another way to express a personal message, with help if they choose.

This brief focuses on adult communication and everyday choice. Age, reading ability, speech, and support needs vary.

2. The problem and its evidence

Unfamiliar people may miss the message

Speech that family understands may be harder for someone else to follow.

Speech does not show everything a person knows

Difficulty being understood can hide an idea, question, joke, or choice.

Support affects who gets heard

Waiting, offering another way to communicate, and checking meaning can help participation.

Adult learning needs more attention

The saved evidence includes limited adult literacy research; child results cannot be assumed to apply to adults.

Fictional everyday example

At a café, a person wants to change an order. Their usual supporter understands them, but the cashier does not. They need a way to show the change and confirm it themselves.

Some sources describe children, professionals, or family views. The adult AAC literacy study involved three adults and reported variable modest gains. It does not establish that the product proposed here teaches reading.

Proposed first version: My words for today. A board for one chosen situation allows personal messages and corrections while keeping preparation short.

4. What the first version includes

The first version supports one complete task: An adult who wants another way to express a personal message, with help if they choose. The screens below define its scope.

Deferred or excluded: Speech treatment, reading improvement claims, guessing choices from taps or silence, and helper-only ownership.

5. Screen-by-screen plan

1

Choose today’s situation

Use a plain title or a personal photo. Start with an empty board; do not assign a board from a diagnosis.

2

Add my words

Add text and an optional photo. A helper can type, but the person gets a review step. Include a way to add an unplanned message.

3

Use the board

Show selected messages with large controls. Offer “Something else,” “That is wrong,” and “Please wait.” Voice output is optional.

4

Keep or clear

Preview a printed page or save selected messages. Let the person remove an outing without removing other boards.

6. Product features and checks

IDRequired behaviourHow to check it
DS-01Use personal words and imagesMake a board without stock symbols or a fixed request list.
DS-02Allow correctionAfter choosing a message, replace it with “That is wrong” and a different message.
DS-03Review help from othersA helper adds text; the review screen clearly invites the person to keep, change, or remove it.
DS-04Print useful backupThe printout includes chosen words and photos at a readable size, with no editing controls.

These are requirements for a future implementation. A listed check has not passed merely because it appears here.

7. Rules every first version must meet

IDRequirementExpected behaviour
C-01Keep control of sharingShow exactly what will be printed, copied, or downloaded. Hidden notes never appear. Sending remains a separate user action.
C-02Explain savingShow “Saved on this device,” “Not saved,” or a specific save error. A browser on a shared device is not a private account.
C-03Handle lost or damaged saved dataKeep the app usable if stored data is malformed. Offer a fresh start and preserve any current unsaved text for download.
C-04Make deletion clearDistinguish removing an item from clearing the whole saved project. Offer undo for the current action; clear that undo copy when the session ends. Explain that exported copies remain outside the app.
C-05Make the flow usableCheck keyboard navigation, visible focus, labelled fields, 200% zoom, and a narrow phone screen. Use text as well as colour for status.
C-06Keep examples optionalStart blank or edit examples. No required name, diagnosis, employer, or explanation for stopping.
C-07Keep data on the deviceThe first version has no accounts, analytics, or remote model calls. Check any speech feature separately before making an offline or privacy promise.
C-08Handle repeat actionsRepeated Save or Download clicks must not change content or create contradictory status. Returning from preview preserves the draft.

8. Information, saving, and privacy

Board title, user-provided text and optional images, order, and selected output. Avoid storing diagnosis, assessment scores, or a record of every message used.

Keep temporary work in memory until the user chooses Save. In version one, saved work remains until the person deletes it or the browser clears it; say this clearly. Closing the page must release temporary drafts and undo copies. Do not promise secure erasure from the device or recall of printed and shared files.

Use a versioned file format for downloads intended for reopening. If reopening files is included, validate the format and ask before replacing an open draft. Treat imported text as text so it cannot run as page code.

9. When something goes wrong

If voice output fails, keep the message visible. If an image cannot load, keep its text label. Large image files need a clear size message and a text-only path.

Cancellation returns to the same draft. An empty field gets a specific explanation where it is required; optional fields remain optional. Losing connection must not discard text already entered. Test the exact offline behaviour promised by the selected build.

10. How we would test usefulness

Can the person express an intended message and correct a mistaken one? Compare with their current communication method; record whether preparing the board added useful help.

  • Prepare a personal café order with one change.
  • Tell the listener the first choice was wrong.
  • Use a printed version with the device unavailable.

First run the fictional tasks as engineering checks. Then prepare an accessible review with intended users and any support they choose. Compare with their existing method. Record task result, errors, help required, and effort in their own words. Choose the first review group for varied relevant needs and report its actual size; no participant results exist for these briefs.

Continue only if the intended task works and the person finds a benefit worth the added effort. Revise confusing wording or steps. Change direction if an existing tool works as well with less effort.

Planning estimate: Medium. This is a relative scope judgment, not a time or budget quote. Review with adults who have Down syndrome and a communication specialist; account for image rights and access methods.

12. Who owns it and how it might reach people

The person chooses the content. A family member or support worker may assist. Adult services are possible partners, but their convenience is not the success measure.

Before estimating a business case, ask who currently handles this task, what they use, how often they need help, who would maintain the tool, and whether anyone would pay. The saved public posts do not answer those questions.

13. What could change the plan

Which text, images, or other methods does each person actually want to use?

Also test whether preparation is too much work, the chosen access method fits, and another person or service must change for the product to help. A software fix may cover only part of the problem.
