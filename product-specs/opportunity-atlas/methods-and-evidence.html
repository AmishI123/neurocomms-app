Methods and evidence

How to read the claims on this atlas

The pages combine public community signals with research and official information. Those sources answer different questions and are labelled separately.

What each source can tell us

Source Useful for Cannot establish by itself
First-person or community post What one person says happened, what help they ask for, and words people use for a problem. How common the problem is, whether a product works, or what every person in a group wants.
Research study What a defined sample and method found in a specific setting. That the finding applies to every person or that a product will work outside the study.
Official guidance Recommended practice, access routes, or service information from the named organisation. Independent proof that a recommendation produces a particular outcome.
Provider or company page What a service or device says it offers, including current features or prices. Independent evidence that the service is effective or that its claimed problem is widespread.

Current source map

The initial source map and one record per important claim are stored as files:

  • sources.json — source title, area, type, URL, and year;
  • evidence-ledger.json — what each source supports and does not support;
  • research-summary.md — first synthesis and candidate products;
  • comparison-scan.md — the five additional areas considered;
  • theme-coding.md and open-questions.md — coding rules and unresolved research questions;
  • NEXT-MEATY-GOAL.md — the full research and production plan.

The atlas is a first product-research release, not a completed review. The five comparison areas now have detailed HTML pages, but their evidence bases remain smaller and more uneven than the ADHD Reddit corpus. Direct adult and self-advocate evidence in Down syndrome and AAC remains a priority for the next pass.

Rules for the next research pass

  • Record who reported each problem.
  • Separate children, teenagers, and adults where the setting or control differs.
  • Include people who communicate through AAC directly wherever accessible.
  • Do not use a caregiver’s interpretation as proof of a person’s current preference.
  • Do not report public-post counts as the percentage of a population.
  • Keep proposed product features clearly labelled as proposals.
  • Record negative results, missing evidence, and access limitations.

What these pages do not claim

They do not diagnose anyone, prove a treatment works, establish clinical benefit, establish market demand, or replace qualified communication, education, or healthcare support. They define product questions worth testing.
