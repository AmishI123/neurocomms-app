# Theme coding rules

This file records how the next research pass will group evidence. It prevents a page from turning a collection of similar words into a claim about a whole population.

## Record-level fields

For every item, record:

- who is speaking: person with the condition, AAC user, caregiver, teacher, clinician, employer, provider, or researcher;
- age group: child, teenager, adult, older adult, mixed, or unknown;
- setting: home, school, work, healthcare, public service, community, online, or mixed;
- communication method: speech, sign, writing, symbols, AAC, mixed, or unknown;
- problem described in the source's own terms;
- possible effect: access, time, effort, privacy, money, safety, social connection, autonomy, or wellbeing;
- requested help or current workaround;
- whether the source describes a tested solution, a personal preference, a provider claim, or an open question.

## Main themes

### Access

The person cannot enter, use, understand, or complete an activity because of the setting, format, device, timing, or rules.

### Expression

The person cannot communicate a thought, need, refusal, question, story, feeling, or identity in the way and time they want.

### Comprehension

Information is too fast, dense, abstract, poorly formatted, or missing the context needed to understand it.

### Production

The person knows what they want to say or do but has difficulty producing speech, text, movement, numbers, or an action.

### Partner or system barrier

Another person, organisation, device, funding route, or service process prevents access or assumes the person can adapt without support.

### Control and privacy

The person cannot choose what is shared, who sees it, which method is used, or whether to continue.

### Continuity

Support disappears when the person changes place, partner, device, battery, service, age group, or life stage.

## Coding rules

- One source may receive more than one theme.
- A theme is a description of what the source says, not a diagnosis or cause.
- Do not count a provider page as a first-person account.
- Do not count a caregiver report as the person's current preference.
- Do not use raw theme totals as population percentages.
- Keep an exact quote short and link to the source. Prefer a paraphrase when the quote is not needed.
- Record uncertainty when the source does not make the speaker, setting, or meaning clear.
- A proposed product feature must never be coded as a finding merely because it appears repeatedly in product pages.
