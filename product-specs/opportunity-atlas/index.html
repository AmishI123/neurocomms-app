Everyday problems and possible products

This atlas looks at autism, Down syndrome, dyslexia, and augmentative and alternative communication (AAC). It explains what people may struggle with in daily life and what a product could do to help.

Read this first: the research points to problems worth investigating. It does not prove that one app works, that every person has the same need, or that a product treats a condition.

The central idea

Start with the situation, not the label

Two people with the same diagnosis may need different support. Two people with different diagnoses may face the same barrier, such as a loud waiting room, a hard-to-read document, a fast conversation, or a communication device that is unavailable.

The useful product question is: What is the person trying to do, what makes access difficult right now, and what control should the person keep?

What repeats across the pages

Five product questions

  1. Can the person communicate what they want in the way that works for them?
  2. Can they take enough time to understand, decide, and respond?
  3. Can they change or refuse a suggestion without being treated as difficult?
  4. Can they keep private information and communication under their control?
  5. Does the support still work when a device, person, setting, battery, or internet connection fails?
Possible products

Ideas to compare

Idea Problem it addresses First small version
Communication access card The person cannot quickly explain what conditions or time they need. Editable private cards such as “please wait,” “written information helps,” or user-written alternatives.
Healthcare conversation preparation A fast or inaccessible appointment makes it hard to ask questions or keep control. Questions, preferred communication method, private notes, and a reviewable summary.
Document access workspace A document is too dense, scanned, tiring, or badly formatted. User-controlled read-aloud, line focus, spacing, text extraction, and original-text checking.
AAC continuity and backup The device, vocabulary, battery, repair, or setting fails. Private export, printable backup, personal phrases, and recovery notes that work across devices.
Partner waiting and confirmation tool Other people rush, guess, or speak for the person. Short user-chosen guidance and a confirmation step without compliance scoring.
Where to go next

How to use these pages

Read one area page. For every pain point, ask whether the proposed product reduces the actual barrier or simply adds another task. Then use the comparison page to choose a narrow idea for a prototype.
