# Compare My Visit Notes with ordinary notes

Status: review materials prepared; no participants contacted or sessions completed.

## Question and success rules, fixed before sessions

Does this app help someone keep and return to their intended appointment questions more easily than their usual method?

For each method, record whether all three intended questions remain available, whether the person can handle an interruption and return to a missed question, whether a new question is retained, and whether private text stays out of a chosen summary. A task is complete when these outcomes hold, with any help recorded. A question can remain unanswered and still be successfully retained.

A useful improvement requires a specific task benefit and a participant who considers it worth the effort. Do not infer preference from speed or completion alone. Do not change this definition after seeing results. Record protocol changes and why they were needed.

## People and setting

Propose five to eight autistic adults who already prepare routine appointment notes. This is an exploratory group, not a representative sample. Allow communication support, breaks, written responses, assistive tools, and withdrawal. Do not collect diagnostic proof, medical histories, clinician names, or real appointment content.

Outreach and recruitment need a separate instruction. Do not send the invitation automatically. Confirm the responsible researcher and data contact before any recruitment. The app may need access changes before someone can review it; record that need rather than excluding it from findings.

## Comparison

Ask what the person normally uses. Have them use that method for one fictional task and the app for the other. Alternate the starting method between sessions. Alternate which fictional task goes with the app. Use the same listener script and allow the same support in both conditions. Explain the app without coaching the answers.

Task A: a fictional follow-up visit. Questions: What happens next? Who can I contact? Can I have the next steps in writing? Private note: “I want to ask about a different service later.”

Task B: a fictional appointment at a new clinic. Questions: Where do I arrive? What should I bring? How do I change the time? Private note: “I may ask someone to come with me.”

The listener answers the first question with a clearly fictional administrative answer, then switches to the third. They leave the second unanswered. Ask the participant to add one new question of their choosing. Then ask them to review what remains open and prepare a summary containing the first and third question, without the private note.

After both tasks, discuss effort, what was hard, what helped, what they would change, and which method they would choose. Accept “neither,” “it depends,” and a decision to stop. Never label an unasked question as personal failure.

## Session record

Use the provided blank record. Record observed task outcomes and participant comments separately. Note device, access method, method order, task order, and assistance. Only record time if the person agreed, and use it as context. Do not record audio, video, or screens by default.

## Data handling

Use an arbitrary session code. Store no names in the results file. If contact details are needed for scheduling, keep them separately and delete them after scheduling and any agreed follow-up. Keep consent records separate from task data. Store results in an access-controlled location chosen before recruitment; do not put participant data in this repository.

Proposed retention: delete raw session notes and the code-to-contact mapping within 30 days after the report is agreed. Set the actual report date, deletion date, responsible contact, and withdrawal deadline before recruitment. Explain whether an anonymous summary already shared can be withdrawn. Do not promise deletion from copies beyond the team's control.

## Analysis and decision

Report the number of sessions actually completed, withdrawals, access barriers, method choices, and task outcomes. Use individual cases and observed counts without general population claims. State who did and did not benefit. Explain whether to continue, revise, or stop and what evidence would change that decision.
