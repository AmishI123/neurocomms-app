# Twelve fictional next-appointment journeys

These examples explain expected software behavior. They are not participant stories or evidence of benefit. No real appointment details are used. Read the save status before closing; offline return requires completed setup.

## 1. Carry one unanswered question

Alex saved a first visit with three questions. On Home, Alex chooses Prepare the next appointment, then deliberately opens that earlier visit. Nothing starts checked. Alex selects one unanswered question, reviews its words and creates the new appointment. The earlier visit stays unchanged. The new copy starts Open, with no private answer or history. Alex changes its wording, adds a title and saves. Returning later reopens that exact copy.

## 2. Decide what comes first

Mina brings two earlier questions and adds a new concern in Prepare. She uses Move up to put her new concern first. Review my appointment plan shows that order without declaring anything medically urgent. Mina downloads the exact plan, then saves the visit separately. Downloading alone would not save the visit. After reopening, the order is unchanged.

## 3. Choose communication needs separately

Sam's earlier visit contains a request for written next steps. Selecting questions does not select that request. Sam deliberately checks it, previews the new appointment and creates it. In What helps, Sam edits the words. The request may appear in the appointment plan while marked Show this request, but shared summaries and conversation view still require their own choices. The earlier request is unchanged.

## 4. Leave a closed item out—or bring it deliberately

Lee opens a previous visit containing answered questions and finished next steps. They are inside Show answered or closed items, not mixed into the initial unfinished list. Lee can leave them alone, or reveal and select one to discuss again. A copied answered question starts Open; it does not undo the earlier answer. An empty selection cannot create a misleading empty copied plan. Starting a fresh visit remains available from the previous-visit chooser.

## 5. Cancel instead of replacing open work

Rae has an unsaved appointment open, then starts planning from an earlier visit. The review warns that creating the new appointment replaces the unsaved open visit. Rae cancels and returns to the open visit; the words remain there. After saving or backing them up, Rae can start the next-appointment selection again. The selection dialog is temporary and is not promised to survive closing the browser.

## 6. Stop, close the browser and return offline

Jo creates a next appointment, edits its questions, chooses requests and saves it successfully. After the app reports its offline files are ready, Jo closes the entire browser. On a later offline launch, Home shows counts, not private titles. Jo deliberately opens the saved appointment and reviews the same words and order. Conversation selections are made again; they are not automatically restored for display.

## 7. The earlier source changes during selection

Chris selects an earlier question. In another tab, its saved wording changes before Chris confirms. Creating the appointment is blocked; the selected preview remains available to read. Chris can go back to the choices or review the latest source again. Fresh source review clears the selections so the changed wording must be chosen deliberately. Nothing overwrites the new source or replaces the current open appointment on the failed confirmation.

## 8. Inspect a source that later disappears

Nico opens Where this item came from on a copied question. The app shows captured title/date and, if available, current source wording/state—not private answers or history. Deleting the source later leaves the copy intact. Inspection then explains that the original is unavailable here. It does not attach the copy to another similarly worded item. The short downloaded plan excludes this source context.

## 9. Saving fails

Pat creates and edits a new plan, but browser storage rejects its save. The app says it is not saved and keeps the editor's words. Pat can review and download the short plan even if saved storage cannot be read. A full private visit backup is the recovery option for all visit fields; the short plan is not a complete backup. After storage works again, Pat retries saving and checks the saved status before closing.

## 10. Move both appointments to another browser

Dee saves the old and new appointments and downloads a private device-transfer file. The receiving browser previews it before saving. Dee opens the next appointment there, goes offline after setup and continues. Source inspection uses the source version that travelled with the copy. If the source changes on the receiving browser and the file is transferred back, differing versions stay separate and their links follow the correct version. The sender's earlier notes are not silently replaced.

## 11. Complete the second conversation

Fran opens the saved next appointment and chooses questions for conversation view. A request is selected separately. Fran previews, shows a question, hides it during an interruption and resumes deliberately. Leaving asks before returning to private notes. Fran records an answer and adds a next step after the visit. This does not mark the original appointment's question answered. Preparing another appointment shows the second visit's remaining work without automatically selecting it.

## 12. Review before sharing or printing

Ash reviews the exact short plan on a narrow screen, using the keyboard. Literal characters and long questions remain text, not markup. The preview includes ordered questions, visible requests and next steps with their selected dates/contacts; private notes, answer notes, history and source context are excluded. Ash can copy, download or print the preview. Clipboard refusal offers downloading instead. None of those actions sends anything, saves the visit or records contact.

## Boundaries shared by all journeys

The app has no account, cloud recovery, automatic sending, medical ranking or appointment scheduling. Saved browser data and downloaded files are unencrypted. Independent copies and downloaded files require separate deletion. Responsive browser checks are not physical Android/iOS or screen-reader testing. Native packaging remains separate from this goal.
