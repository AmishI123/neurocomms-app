# Whole-app journeys

These are fictional examples for product review and software tests. They are not participant reports or evidence that the redesign is easier. The acceptance audit must identify which steps were actually tested.

## 1. First opening, no saved work

Mina opens the app and sees Start a visit, Continue work and Follow up. Counts say that nothing is saved here yet. No question, visit title or personal wording appears. Mina chooses Prepare my visit and writes a question. The workspace puts the question before recovery, transfer and installation explanations. Mina can still find those through Tools and settings. Nothing is saved merely by starting.

## 2. Returning home with a temporary visit

Sol writes a title and two questions without saving. Home hides the workspace but does not replace it. The home message explains the temporary state and offers Return to my open visit. Sol returns to the same words and screen. Choosing a new visit still needs the existing replacement warning. Closing the page is different from navigating Home: the temporary work needs saving or a private download first.

## 3. Coming back another day

Jules saved a visit yesterday. Reopening the app shows counts, not the visit's words. Jules explicitly opens saved visits, chooses the intended visit and continues. Returning Home closes the private list. Opening Tools and settings again must not unexpectedly show that earlier expanded list. Saving locally does not create an account or protect notes from someone deliberately using the same browser.

## 4. Preparing from existing notes

Alex opens Bring existing notes, selects a passage and chooses a destination. The choice names a new temporary visit, the open visit or a particular saved visit. The confirmation repeats that destination before adding anything. Source notes remain preparation, not automatically shared visit text. When complete, the selected question joins the normal visit workflow rather than a separate list of questions.

## 5. Changing visits while preparation is unfinished

Rae chooses an open temporary visit as the destination for a passage, then closes preparation and edits that visit. Rae later opens a different visit. The preparation keeps the earlier destination and its latest in-memory copy at replacement; it must not switch to the new visit just because that visit is now open. Review names the earlier destination. Applying to a retained draft still warns before replacing the open visit. No passages are added merely by returning to preparation.

## 6. Resuming saved preparation

Kim saves preparation containing source text and chosen passages, closes the browser and returns offline after setup. Home offers Continue saved preparation. Kim explicitly chooses the saved preparation and checks its destination. Existing completion records prevent already-applied passages from being added again. If the original destination is missing, the app asks for a deliberate new choice rather than guessing.

## 7. Preparing to show chosen words

Dev writes questions and uses What helps to choose communication wording. The workspace explains that conversation view needs its own selection and preview. Dev chooses a question and a request, checks the words and shows them. Private preparation and answer notes remain outside that display. Escape hides the message. Returning to personal notes requires a deliberate exit, then the ordinary visit review remains available.

## 8. Finishing without inventing progress

Lee reviews unanswered questions and personal next steps. The finish section reports their counts without ranking them, marking them done or deciding what matters. It also reports whether the visit is saved and whether other work remains only in this page. Lee may return Home, follow up across saved visits or continue editing. Returning Home does not claim that an unsaved visit was saved.

## 9. Keeping a message while checking something else

Ash builds and edits a follow-up message, closes the dialog and returns Home. The home screen says a temporary message remains but does not show its wording. Return to my message restores the exact edited text. Making another message asks before replacing this one. Discard asks for confirmation and does not delete saved visits. Downloading or copying does not send the message or mark anyone contacted. The draft does not survive page closure.

## 10. Keeping an unsaved progress entry

Jo changes a follow-up state, date and private note but has not saved. Closing the dialog retains a return path on Home. Reopening restores those fields without making an event in saved history. Saving uses the original item and conflict checks; a newer saved change is not silently overwritten. Jo can download the private draft if it cannot be saved. Discarding the draft is separate from deleting previously saved progress.

## 11. Waiting or failing to save

Tari moves Home while a visit save waits on another browser tab. The status remains unsaved until the write actually completes. A storage failure leaves the work in memory and keeps the warning. Returning to the visit must recover the current in-memory text, not an older saved copy. Home counts and navigation must not conceal a conflict or dismiss the browser-close warning.

## 12. Keyboard and smaller screens

Noor uses Home, the visit steps and Tools and settings with the keyboard. Opening a group makes its controls reachable; cancellation returns focus to a useful place. Long text and larger reading choices wrap without horizontal scrolling. A narrow browser view is a software layout check, not proof of physical-phone or screen-reader usability. The full journey needs testing after the package is extracted, not only from the development folder.
