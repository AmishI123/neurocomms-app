PRODUCT DECISION REPORT · 17 SEPTEMBER 2026

Show me the time steps

A small local-first tool that works backward from one appointment and shows when to start preparing and when to leave.

Decision today

Keep the product as a controlled prototype and move it to approved human review. The engineering slice is complete enough to review. It is not ready for claims about benefit, demand, or broad accessibility.

What the product does

Person enters the assumptions

Appointment time, preparation minutes, travel minutes, spare minutes, and display choice.

Product shows the steps

Start preparing, leave, and appointment time, with dates and the arithmetic behind them.

Person stays in control

Changing a value changes the result. The product does not choose a travel time or claim to know readiness.

Work stays local

Save, reload, delete, and print work on the device. There is no account, cloud sync, calendar, traffic service, or remote AI in the product.

What we cannot claim

  • It helps every autistic, dyslexic, ADHD, Down syndrome, or other neurodivergent person.
  • It improves punctuality, reduces stress, or improves health or outcomes.
  • It predicts travel time or confirms that a person is ready to leave.
  • It is clinically safe, medically useful, or a replacement for support.
  • People prefer it to a calculator, paper, or their usual method.
  • It has passed native Android or iOS use.

Open gates

  1. Run the fictional task with an approved accessibility or product reviewer using the review guide.
  2. Review the downloaded response privately and record only the agreed evidence.
  3. Run the native Android journey on an authorized target. The current emulator stayed offline; the runner recorded this and completed zero steps.
  4. Run iOS packaging on an authorized macOS/Xcode environment.
  5. Revise, continue, or stop based on observed confusion, access barriers, privacy concerns, and reviewer recommendation.
