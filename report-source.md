# Research opportunities in neurodivergent child support

Evidence and project brief • 7 September 2026

## A practical starting point for your paper

My recommendation is to explore two questions: how adults can prepare better communication materials, and how children can express preferences about their sensory environment. Both connect an identifiable problem to something you could build and evaluate. Choose between them after checking fit with PRGN; the institute's identity, population and resources are still unknown.

The most promising contribution is evidence about a specific design decision or implementation problem. Existing multilingual communication apps, parent-support systems and personalized sensory technologies already cover the broad categories. This brief identifies questions left unresolved by particular studies; it does not certify that a question has never been studied anywhere.

## What institutes and companies reveal

**Action for Autism, India:** parent–child training, personalized home plans, counseling and respite services address support across everyday family life. These offerings motivate asking how a tool fits routines and caregiver effort. They do not quantify unmet demand at PRGN.

**Avaz:** its communication app supports picture/text vocabulary, Indian languages, customization and partner training. A new multilingual picture board would therefore need a more specific contribution.

**Gabify:** its June 2026 product article describes fragmented records and messages and markets coordinated care software. Treat fragmentation here as a vendor-reported pain point. Independent evidence of its frequency and consequences at PRGN is missing.

**Autistica / NeuInsight:** a university research partnership co-developed training for sensory-inclusive public spaces. This establishes work on environmental accommodations; it does not demonstrate that a particular room change benefits every child.

Sources: [Action for Autism, Family Support & Empowerment, undated](https://www.autism-india.org/family-support-empowerment.php); [Avaz Inc., Avaz AAC, undated](https://avazapp.com/products/avaz-aac-landing-page/); [Anjali, Gabify, What is Connect by Gabify?, June 16, 2026](https://www.gabify.life/blog/what-is-connect-by-gabify); [Autistica, NeuInsight sensory inclusion project, project period 2023–2024](https://www.autistica.org.uk/our-research/research-projects/public-spaces-training-sensory-inclusive).

**Scope:** targeted research through 7 September 2026, emphasizing 2024–2026. The main evidence is autism-specific. ADHD and dyslexia receive separate checks later. Indian organizations are examples, not an assumption about PRGN's location. This is a focused opportunity review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a treatment recommendation.

# Communication: help people use the tools well

## What recent studies actually found

**AACessTalk — CHI 2025.** Eleven child–parent pairs in South Korea used an AI-mediated communication system at home for two weeks. It offered adult guidance and contextual vocabulary cards. The authors reported engagement and changes in conversational interaction. Recruitment selected families able to use the system, including children able to operate a touchscreen and homes with stable Wi-Fi. This was a deployment study, not a randomized efficacy trial. Its limitations include cultural mismatch in recommendations, reliance on parents' interpretations and no direct child satisfaction evaluation. Sustained, child-valued benefit in other settings remains unresolved by this study.

Source: Dasom Choi and colleagues, [AACessTalk: Fostering Communication between Minimally Verbal Autistic Children and Parents with Contextual Guidance and Card Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09641v4), CHI 2025; inspected revised full text, February 14, 2025.

**POWR+ — online 2024, issue 2026.** A multiple-probe single-case study involved five US teacher–paraeducator–child teams. Two teams implementing the training with high fidelity showed functional relationships for communication opportunities and AAC modeling; findings for the other teams were mixed. This points toward implementation support as a research direction. It does not prove that implementation fidelity alone caused the difference.

Source: Sarah N. Douglas and colleagues, [POWR+ Intervention Results: An Online Communication Partner Training Program for Educational Teams](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15257401241303160), Communication Disorders Quarterly; December 26, 2024 online, February 2026 issue. Abstract inspected; full text unavailable.

**Helping or Homogenizing? — ASSETS 2025.** Sixteen pre-service speech-language pathologists were analyzed in a within-person comparison of AI-assisted visual-scene creation and Snap Scene. AI assistance improved preparation efficiency and confidence, while communication options became more homogeneous and quality findings were mixed. Children were not participants. A tool can make adult preparation faster without demonstrating better communication for its intended users.

Source: Cynthia Zastudil and colleagues, [Helping or Homogenizing? GenAI as a Design Partner to Pre-Service SLPs for Just-in-Time Programming of AAC](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21811v1), ASSETS 2025; July 29, 2025 preprint, full text inspected.

## The bounded opportunity

Study whether an adult review interface preserves relevant, varied, personalized choices while keeping preparation manageable. This is an extension of an identified design problem. A claim of global novelty would require a narrower follow-up search after fixing the interface, users and comparison.

# Sensory environments: measure comfort carefully

## Two findings that change the project design

**Classroom displays and noise — 2026.** Jones and colleagues studied 25 autistic children aged 8–11 and 22 comparison children aged 6–11 in a mini-classroom with four reading conditions. Visual displays increased coded off-task behavior, especially among autistic participants. The expected negative effect of recorded classroom noise was not found. Brief tasks, selected independent readers and a controlled setting limit transfer to ordinary institute activities. The outcome was engagement, not comfort: neither a noise threshold nor a universal room prescription follows from this experiment.

Source: Elizabeth Jones and colleagues, [The Effect of the Classroom Sensory Environment on Engagement for Autistic Pupils: Classroom Noise and Classroom Displays](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27546330261443570), Neurodiversity, April 21, 2026. Research-lane full text inspected; coordinating check used indexed primary text after direct fetch failures.

**Different perspectives — 2024/2025.** Rajuan and colleagues compared clinician, parent, teacher and child sensory measures in 30 autistic and 27 comparison children aged 6–10 in Israel. Adult reports and child pleasantness ratings did not yield the same pattern. The authors suggest children might have interpreted a smile-face scale as task success rather than pleasantness. That explanation is a possibility, not a demonstrated cause. The selected Hebrew-proficient sample also limits generalization.

Source: Gili Rajuan, Lihi Liberman and Orit Bart, [Sensory Processing in Children With Autistic Spectrum Disorder: Clinicians’, Parents’, Teachers’ and Children’s Perspectives](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10883576241282302), Focus on Autism and Other Developmental Disabilities; October 4, 2024 online, 2025 issue. Full text inspected.

## Personalized technology already exists

A 2026 study enrolled 83 autistic and 50 comparison children aged 7–12 and developed adjustable noise-canceling headphones with a mobile app. Its abstract reports that after three weeks, 18 parents observed improved reactions to sensitive sounds and six saw no change. Those 24 reports are not outcomes for all 83 autistic children. Full methods were embargoed, preventing assessment of comparative efficacy. The study demonstrates an existing research direction, not a product recommendation.

Source: T. C. Kwong and colleagues, [Auditory-perception-based tuneable noise control approach for improving acoustic comfort and behaviour of autistic individuals in indoor/outdoor environments](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/117452?mode=full), Building and Environment 290, 114179, February 15, 2026. Institutional abstract inspected.

**Opportunity:** investigate how interpretable, voluntary child feedback can guide ordinary environmental choices. An environmental logger measures sound or light; it cannot establish a child's internal state.

# Project A: reviewable communication materials

## A concrete software and paper question

**Question:** For staff preparing communication materials, does a brief personalization review improve relevance and variety compared with the same editable suggestions without that review?

The rationale is the efficiency–quality tradeoff in Zastudil and colleagues' study. The proposed experiment below is our design sketch; it is not a tested intervention. [ASSETS 2025 source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21811v1).

**Prototype:** an authoring screen where adults edit suggested phrases, see the fictional person's stated interests and vocabulary, and check whether the set allows more than requests—for example commenting, disagreement and social connection. Preserve manual entry and make suggestions clearly optional. Begin with fixed example suggestions so the interface can be evaluated separately from model variability.

**Comparison:** give each adult matched fictional activity scenarios in two conditions: the same editor with and without the review step. Counterbalance condition order and scenario assignment. Keep preparation instructions and available information constant. Have a qualified communication professional help define a scoring rubric before testing, with raters unaware of the condition where feasible.

**Primary outcome:** a prespecified relevance score against each fictional scenario. Secondary outcomes: preparation time, inappropriate suggestions, communication functions represented, perceived effort and edits made. More phrases are not automatically better. A separate rating of variety prevents a relevance score from hiding repetitive choices.

**Analysis:** use the adult participant as the unit for paired comparisons; multiple phrases from one person are not independent participants. Report individual differences and uncertainty. Choose sample size with a mentor based on the target claim and resources; a convenience pilot supports feasibility conclusions, not a general efficacy claim.

**What a useful result looks like:** better-rated materials with acceptable effort, or evidence that the checklist adds time without improving quality. Either informs design. Adult ratings alone cannot prove child benefit or identify a child's preferred vocabulary.

## Feasibility and first build

This route needs coding, a speech-language or AAC mentor and access to adult reviewers. Before research participation begins, a static prototype and fictional scenario set can be developed and checked by the student. Recorded adult usability research still needs the applicable review process.

**Working paper title:** “Evaluating a personalization review step for AI-assisted communication-material preparation.” This is a proposed title, not a claim of results.

# Project B: a child-directed room preference tool

## A question linking communication and environment

**Question:** Which concrete feedback format best enables each participating child to express whether they want an ordinary room feature kept or changed?

Start with comprehension and usability. Rajuan and colleagues' rating-scale limitation makes that question more defensible than immediately training an algorithm to infer discomfort. The proposed formats and outcomes below are design ideas, not validated clinical measures. [Sensory perspectives study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10883576241282302).

**Prototype:** a simple interface using familiar pictures or existing AAC vocabulary, with options such as “keep,” “change,” “not sure” and “stop.” The child can identify what they mean through their established communication method. Avoid rewarding a particular response. A changed answer may reflect a changed preference; it is not automatically a measurement error.

**First study phase, with a mentor and appropriate review:** compare formats during ordinary activities without creating aversive stimuli. Record understanding of the question, effort, requests for assistance, voluntary skips and whether the adult understood the requested change. Adult interpretation should be recorded separately from the child's expression. Agreement with an adult is not the ground truth for comfort.

**Optional engineering component:** build a room-level sound/light logger and test it in an unoccupied setting against reference instruments. Record placement, measurement range, error, missing readings and repeatability. Process sound into level measurements without retaining intelligible audio. If calibration is unavailable, report relative readings rather than labeling arbitrary values as accurate decibels.

**Later extension:** pair voluntary preference reports with environmental readings during ordinary activities. Keep analysis within each child before considering group patterns. Control or document activity, time, occupancy and location. Repeated readings are not independent children. Association between a reading and a request does not establish a causal sensory threshold.

## What the project could contribute

A usable feedback interface, a transparent engineering evaluation, and evidence about whether staff can act on expressed preferences. This concerns accommodation and communication access; it does not establish a treatment that changes sensory processing.

This is the stronger route if you want hardware and sustained involvement with the institute. It depends more heavily on suitable mentorship and participant access than Project A. A sensor benchmark can start independently, but cannot substitute for the eventual child-centered question.

**Working paper title:** “Supporting children's room preferences through accessible feedback and environmental measurement.”

# Other routes worth keeping open

## ADHD: study everyday use and meaningful comparisons

ParentCoach's 2025 design paper used secondary qualitative material from over 30 families and expert co-design. It emphasized brief, flexible interactions and acknowledged representation limits for non-English-speaking and digitally excluded families. The paper did not report a completed efficacy evaluation. This supports studying burden and contextual fit, without assuming a new parent-coaching app is novel.

Source: Franceli L. Cibrian and colleagues, [ParentCoach: designing an mHealth parenting app to enhance parental involvement in ADHD support](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1694747/full), Frontiers in Computer Science, November 21, 2025; full text inspected. No later efficacy report was verified in this review.

A 2025 pilot randomized 31 middle-school students with ADHD to a game-supported or traditional organizational-skills intervention, both with teacher mentoring for up to 24 weeks. Between-condition interactions were small and inconclusive. Improvement over time alone did not establish that adding the game was better.

Source: Brandon K. Schultz and colleagues, [A pilot study of a game-supported organization and planning skills intervention for young adolescents with ADHD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40506186/), Journal of School Psychology, June 2025; online May 9. Abstract inspected.

**Possible question:** does a child-chosen task preview reduce effort in beginning an ordinary activity compared with the institute's existing preview? Only pursue this if those routines and needs are locally relevant; do not substitute a game score for everyday benefit.

## Dyslexia: check who the evidence is about

The 2025 LARF AI-annotation study recruited 150 adults, mean age 36.8, with diagnosed, suspected or in-process dyslexia. Reading-time differences were not significant. Its adult English-reading evaluation cannot establish benefit for children or Indian-language learning. A child-reading project would need a distinct evidence review and age-appropriate measures.

Source: Sihang Zhao and colleagues, [Let AI Read First: Enhancing Reading Abilities for Individuals with Dyslexia through Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00941v1), CHI 2025 Late Breaking Work; April 1, 2025 preprint, full text inspected.

## Biology and wet-lab work

Keep this as a separate mentor-led route. A concrete computational starting point is to investigate how robust a published gene-set conclusion is to evidence thresholds or dataset choice, before considering laboratory validation. SFARI Gene offers curated gene information and downloadable data. Its gene lists are distinct from controlled participant-level resources in SFARI Base.

Sources: [SFARI Gene, Tools and data download](https://gene.sfari.org/tools/); [SFARI, SFARI Base access requirements](https://www.sfari.org/resource/sfari-base/), undated official pages.

This is a proposed reproducibility project, not an identified new biological mechanism. A wet-lab experiment remains unscoped: choose the model, question and supervision with a qualified laboratory mentor. Molecular findings would not by themselves explain a particular child's daily support needs.

# How to turn this into a credible project

## Choose using the contribution you want to make

**Software and a manageable first study:** Project A. Its contribution would be evidence about preparation quality and effort. Child outcomes remain a later question.

**Hardware and institute-centered design:** Project B. Its contribution would combine measurement quality with usable expression of preferences. The engineering and participant-study claims need separate evidence.

**Biological research:** a mentor-guided reproducibility study followed, if justified and feasible, by laboratory work. This has the weakest direct connection to the institute's daily pain points in the current review.

These priorities reflect feasibility, not clinical superiority.

## A proposed sequence, adjustable to your timeline

1. Define the users, one activity, existing support and one meaningful outcome. Keep informal institute contact separate from research records.
2. With a mentor, inspect the closest papers and tools. Write a question, hypothesis, primary outcome and limitations. Label replication or local adaptation honestly.
3. Build a fictional-data prototype or unoccupied-room benchmark. Prespecify what would count against the hypothesis.
4. Obtain applicable review before recruitment, interviews or participant testing. Run the approved study; report burden, missing data and individual variation.
5. Write around what was actually tested. Distinguish usability, sustained use and effectiveness.

For an ISEF-affiliated project, current Society for Science rules require prior IRB review for human-participant studies, including most prototype tests with other people; minors require assent and written parental permission. Adult staff studies are not automatically exempt. These fair rules are not a substitute for local institutional requirements.

Source: Society for Science, [Human Participants — International Rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/), current page checked September 7, 2026. Applicability to this user's project is not yet known.

## What remains uncertain

PRGN's population, tools, priorities, resources and timeline are unknown. Selected autism samples cannot represent all neurodivergent children. Several sources were abstract-only. Product descriptions establish offerings, not effectiveness. No study or prototype has been completed for this project.

The review combined targeted searches, primary-source follow-up and independent communication/sensory evidence lanes. It stopped when evidence distinguished feasible questions and their limits. No exhaustive database screening or formal risk-of-bias assessment was performed. Searches extended to September 7, 2026; complete coverage is not claimed.
