Helper Jobs · an early test app

Give someone a job they can be proud of. A volunteer is always close by.

For Pragnya adults who help at kids’ Sunday outings, and the volunteers who run them.

What gets in the way

Four moments where helping gets hard. The hard part is the moment, not the person.

  • Having a real role

    “Help out” is not a job

    Without a clear task, it is easy to stand at the side, or to be handed jobs that are not real.

    Helper Jobs: six real jobs, each with a start, a middle and a done.

  • Following the steps

    Spoken instructions are hard to hold on to

    In a busy park, instructions come fast and all at once.

    Helper Jobs: one picture step per screen, a few words, and a button that reads it aloud.

  • Keeping kids safe

    The lead tracks who is with which kid

    Snacks, allergies, roads and bathroom breaks all need an adult’s decision.

    Helper Jobs: kid jobs need a named volunteer nearby. No snack means no snack.

  • Getting help

    When something goes wrong, help must be quick

    A child runs off, or a helper feels unsure, and there is no time to find the words.

    Helper Jobs: a big Help button on every screen. It reaches the lead’s phone in about a second.

AlwaysHelpers are never alone with a kid, and never give bathroom or changing help. No snack means no snack.

How it works

One shared tablet, three roles. Kids never use it.

  • Coordinator

    Keeps the records

    Helpers’ levels and approved jobs

    Kids’ tips, snack cards and consent

    Approves new jobs; nothing is automatic

  • Volunteer lead

    Runs the day

    Ticks who is here today

    Matches helper, kid and a volunteer nearby

    Gets Help on their own phone, in about a second

  • Helper

    Does a real job

    Taps their picture, no login

    One picture step at a time, read aloud

    Help and Stop always one tap away

Six real jobs

Start with three. New jobs unlock only when a coordinator says ready.

What we don’t know yet: no outing has used Helper Jobs. Linked phones and tablets need internet, and we have not tried them in a busy park. The Hindi and Spanish need a native speaker’s check. The first test is three Sundays with three to five adult helpers.
