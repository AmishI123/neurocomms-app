What this is

Helper Jobs gives Pragnya adults a real job at kids’ Sunday outings, with a volunteer always close by.

Pick your picture, pick a job, and follow one picture step at a time. Help is one tap away, and stopping is always okay. Nothing happens unless you tap.

One shared tablet. No logins. Kids never use it.

What gets in the way

Four moments on a Sunday outing where helping gets hard. The hard part is the moment, not the person.

  1. Having a real role

    “Help out” is not a job

    Without a clear task, it is easy to stand at the side, or to be handed jobs that are not real.

    Helper Jobs: six real jobs, each with a start, a middle and a done.

  2. Following the steps

    Spoken instructions are hard to hold on to

    In a busy park, instructions come fast and all at once.

    Helper Jobs: one picture step per screen, a few words, and a button that reads it aloud.

  3. Keeping kids safe

    The lead tracks who is with which kid

    Snacks, allergies, roads and bathroom breaks all need an adult’s decision.

    Helper Jobs: kid jobs need a named volunteer nearby. A “no snack” card can’t be changed on the day.

  4. Getting help

    When something goes wrong, help has to be quick

    A child runs off, or a helper feels unsure, and there is no time to find the words.

    Helper Jobs: a big Help button on every screen, and Stop is always okay. It reaches the lead’s phone in about a second, and stays there until they answer.

Under all of it, dignity: helpers earn stars and badges for real work, and take on new jobs only when a coordinator says they are ready. Helpers are never alone with a kid, and never give bathroom or changing help.

What this can’t tell us yet: no outing has used Helper Jobs. Next: three Sundays with three to five adult helpers, and listening to what makes the day easier.

Built for the whole day

From who is here in the morning to every kid back at the end, and what to do if the tablet stops.

  • On the day

    Break reminders

    After 20 minutes on one job, the board and the lead’s phone say “offer a break”.

  • Wrap-up

    Every kid back

    The outing can’t finish until every kid is marked back with a volunteer or home with family.

  • For every helper

    Their language, their pace

    Steps and read aloud in English, Hindi or Spanish. Bigger text, stronger contrast, a slower voice.

  • More devices

    One outing, many tablets

    A second helper tablet, the lead’s phone and a laptop backup. All locked before anything is sent.

  • If the tablet stops

    Paper backup

    Printed job cards with the same pictures, and a sheet for matches and the head count.

  • Families

    Only with a yes

    A family page with one helper’s stars, badges and certificates, in English, Hindi or Spanish. Photos only after a yes.

  • The day plan

    Now and next

    Picture steps for the day with times. Helpers see what is happening now and what comes next.

  • For the coordinator

    Make a job for a new place

    A zoo or a library: picture steps, a real photo of the place, locked until a helper is ready.

  • After each Sunday

    One page to learn from

    Jobs done, how fast help came, how each job felt and what people said. No kid names.

Where kids’ details go

They stay with the coordinator on the main tablet. Helpers see only a first name, a picture letter and up to three tips. A paired phone or second tablet gets first names, jobs and alerts, locked on the tablet first. Photos never leave the main tablet.

What it is not

It does not replace a volunteer, and it does not diagnose anything. Helpers are still told to find their volunteer when they press Help, because a phone can be in a pocket.
